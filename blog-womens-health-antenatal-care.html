Women's Health & Antenatal Care: A Physiotherapy Guide

Pelvic health, pregnancy-safe movement, postnatal recovery and everyday wellness for women at every stage of life.

By AlignAsya Physiotherapy Team · August 2026

Women's bodies go through remarkable changes — through pregnancy, childbirth, motherhood and the natural shifts of every decade. Yet many of these changes are treated as something to simply "put up with." Back ache in pregnancy, leaking on a sneeze, pelvic heaviness, or a core that never quite feels the same after childbirth — these are common, but they are not normal to live with. Physiotherapy for women's health exists to help you move, recover and feel strong through all of it.

What is women's health physiotherapy?

Women's health physiotherapy is a specialised area of physiotherapy focused on the pelvic floor, core stability, posture and movement patterns that change through pregnancy, childbirth and the postpartum period. It also supports women managing pelvic pain, incontinence, prolapse, diastasis recti and the musculoskeletal changes that come with hormonal shifts across the lifespan.

Your body is not "broken" after pregnancy — it has adapted. Physiotherapy helps it adapt well, recover well and feel strong again.

Antenatal care: supporting your body through pregnancy

Pregnancy is a time of rapid change. As your baby grows, your centre of gravity shifts forward, your ligaments soften under the influence of relaxin, and your abdominal muscles stretch and separate. These changes are natural — but they can also lead to lower back pain, pelvic girdle pain, breathlessness and fatigue if the body isn't supported with the right movement.

1. Pelvic girdle pain and lower back care

Many women experience pain around the pelvis or lower back during pregnancy, especially in the second and third trimesters. A physiotherapist can assess your posture, movement patterns and pelvic alignment, then guide you with gentle stabilisation exercises, supportive taping and daily movement strategies that reduce strain.

2. Safe, pregnancy-adapted exercise

Staying active during pregnancy supports circulation, energy, sleep and mood — and can even ease labour. The key is choosing movements that respect your changing body. Physiotherapy-guided antenatal exercise focuses on gentle strengthening of the glutes, deep core and pelvic floor, while avoiding positions that overstress the abdomen or pelvis.

3. Breathing and posture for comfort

As the uterus grows, the diaphragm has less room to move, which can make breathing feel shallow. Learning to breathe with your pelvic floor and diaphragm together supports both comfort and core function. Simple posture adjustments — in sitting, standing and sleeping — can also relieve a surprising amount of pregnancy-related discomfort.

Postnatal recovery: rebuilding from the inside out

The weeks and months after childbirth are a critical window for recovery. Your pelvic floor has been through a great deal, your abdominal muscles may have separated (diastasis recti), and your body is adjusting to new demands — feeding, carrying, bending, lifting. Postnatal physiotherapy helps you rebuild strength safely and progressively.

Pelvic floor rehabilitation

The pelvic floor supports your bladder, bowel and uterus. After childbirth, it can become weakened, leading to incontinence or a feeling of heaviness. A physiotherapist can assess your pelvic floor function and guide you through a personalised programme of activation, strengthening and relaxation — because a pelvic floor that is too tight can be just as problematic as one that is too weak.

Diastasis recti and core restoration

Diastasis recti — the separation of the abdominal muscles — is common in pregnancy and often persists after birth. It is not something to "fix" with crunches. A physiotherapist can teach you how to reconnect with your deep core, coordinate your breathing with your pelvic floor, and progress safely back to full strength.

Returning to exercise after birth

Many new mothers are eager to return to running, gym classes or sport. Doing so too quickly — without rebuilding pelvic floor and core capacity — can lead to pain, leakage or pelvic organ prolapse. A structured, physio-guided return to exercise respects your body's timeline and helps you build back with confidence.

Beyond pregnancy: women's health across the lifespan

Women's health physiotherapy is not only for pregnancy and postpartum. It also supports women through perimenopause and menopause, when hormonal changes can affect joint comfort, bone density, pelvic floor function and overall movement confidence. It can help with pelvic pain conditions, post-surgical recovery (such as after hysterectomy or caesarean section), and the musculoskeletal changes that come with every stage of life.

When should you see a women's health physiotherapist?

You don't need to wait for a problem to become severe. Consider a women's health physiotherapy assessment if you experience any of the following:

  • Back or pelvic pain during pregnancy
  • Leaking urine when you cough, sneeze, laugh or exercise
  • A feeling of heaviness or bulging in the pelvic area
  • Diastasis recti or a "gap" in your abdominal muscles after birth
  • Pain during or after intercourse
  • Difficulty returning to exercise after childbirth
  • Pelvic pain or discomfort that isn't improving on its own

Care that respects your story

Every woman's body, history and goals are different. Whether you are planning a pregnancy, currently expecting, recently delivered, or navigating the changes of midlife, a women's health physiotherapist meets you where you are — with a thorough assessment, a personalised plan and a supportive, judgement-free approach.

Quick Women's Health Tips

  • Pelvic floor exercises are as important during pregnancy as after — start early and stay consistent.
  • Listen to your body: pain, heaviness or leaking are signs to seek a physiotherapy assessment.
  • Return to exercise gradually after birth — rebuilding your core and pelvic floor comes first.
