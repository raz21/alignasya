Cupping Therapy
in Mira Road, Mumbai

Suction cups create gentle negative pressure on the skin to promote blood flow, ease muscle tightness and support recovery. Offered by Dr. Renu Saroj and her team at AlignAsya as part of a complete treatment plan.

What It's Used For

When Cupping Can Help

Muscle Tightness

Easing persistently tight muscles that respond well to increased local blood flow.

Chronic Back & Neck Pain

Complementing manual therapy for long-standing tension patterns.

Sports Recovery

Supporting muscle recovery between training sessions or after competition.

Restricted Tissue Mobility

Helping tight fascia and connective tissue move more freely.

General Muscular Fatigue

Easing the heavy, fatigued feeling that builds up in overworked muscles.

Complementary Pain Relief

Used alongside other modalities as part of a broader rehabilitation plan.

Our Approach

A Complementary, Whole-Plan Approach

Cupping works best as part of a wider treatment plan rather than a standalone fix. Our physiotherapists use it selectively, alongside manual therapy and exercise, based on what your assessment shows will genuinely help.

  • ✓ Assessment-led selection of cupping sites
  • ✓ Pressure adjusted to your comfort level
  • ✓ Combined with manual therapy and exercise
  • ✓ Clear aftercare guidance
  • ✓ Adjusted or skipped for sensitive skin when appropriate
Patient Knowledge Base

Cupping Therapy FAQ

The suction can bring blood to the surface of the skin, leaving temporary circular marks that typically fade within a few days to a week. This is a normal, expected response and not a sign of injury.

Most people describe it as a tight, pulling sensation rather than pain. Suction strength is adjusted to your comfort throughout the session.

This depends on your condition and how your skin responds. Your physiotherapist will space out sessions appropriately, often allowing marks to fade before repeating treatment on the same area.

Cupping isn't suitable for everyone — people with certain skin conditions, blood-clotting concerns or over broken skin should avoid it. Your physiotherapist will screen for this during your assessment.
